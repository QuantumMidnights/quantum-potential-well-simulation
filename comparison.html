Classical vs Quantum

Explore how the two models describe a particle in a well. Adjust the parameters, compare the energy descriptions, and explain what changes.

MODEL A

Classical particle

In this simplified classical model, the particle has a definite position and velocity. Its energy is a continuous parameter rather than a set of quantized levels.

  • We can choose a range of energy values.
  • The particle follows a trajectory in the model.
  • Changing the box length alone does not force the selected classical energy to a particular value.

Quantum particle in a box

The quantum model describes the particle using a wavefunction. For an ideal one-dimensional infinite box, the allowed stationary-state energies depend on quantum number and box length.

Key idea

Classical: the model allows a continuous range of energy values. Quantum: the ideal particle-in-a-box model has discrete allowed energy levels, with Eₙ ∝ n²/L². These statements apply to the simplified models described here.
